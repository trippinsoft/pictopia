# Pictopia

Keep the memories. Lose the clutter.

Pictopia is an Expo SDK 55 / React Native 0.83 application with a working browser review flow and native media-library, SQLite, local-analysis, and RevenueCat integrations. **It is not yet verified for commercial release.** Native build compilation, device photo-library/deletion tests, and genuine store sandbox purchases remain required.

## Adventures (Home and Explore)

The tabs are Home, Explore and Settings. **Home** is where Pictopia chooses: two equally weighted Adventures, A and B, each a short trip of about 18 photos with an intriguing title and a hint that is always true of what's inside (dates, screenshot flags and timestamps only; never places, people or themes, which the app doesn't know). The pair is picked together by an explainable curation engine that scores candidates for discovery, coherence, variety, cleanup potential, effort and your feedback, so A and B are never near-twins. One tap opens the vertical explorer: scroll vertically in either direction without making decisions, and swipe a photo sideways to send it to the trash — it flies out of your hand into the trashcan in the corner and leaves the scrolling sequence. Undo brings the last one straight back; tapping the trashcan opens a tray of everything this adventure has marked, where Restore puts a photo back exactly where it was. Screen readers can use photo actions for navigation, marking and Undo. Leave anytime and Home offers **Continue**. **Explore** is where you choose: Time Machine (a year or month), On This Day, Surprise Me and Screenshot Sweep, shown only when your library supports them. Scroll forward beyond the final photo, or mark the final original photo, to finish an Adventure with a short closing line and what you explored and set aside. New discard marks enter the existing deletion queue atomically with session state; they never delete an original. The trashcan count is this adventure's pending marks, the tray and the deletion queue are the same list, and actual deletion still requires explicit queue confirmation and OS approval. Older adventure-only set-aside marks are preserved without auto-enqueuing them. Settings shows your Journey: photos explored, decided, set aside, queued, deleted, and space recovered (verified deletions only). A developer-only **Adventure Lab** opens when you long-press the version line in Settings.

Home, Explore, Pit Stops, Settings and Adventure completion share the standard warm ivory/deep teal light and dark themes, Fraunces headings, and a theme-aware SVG illustration system; photo review alone uses the black Adventure theme. Time Machine, On This Day, and Detour use optimized photorealistic background artwork on Home and their finish screens, with separate white-bordered cards showing real photos saved in that Adventure when available. Each card follows its photo's portrait or landscape shape and shows the full image; Detour stacks up to three different photos on its cover. Other themes retain their painted artwork; Duplicates artwork is ready for a future Duplicates Adventure. The artwork contains no personal photos itself. Home’s **Show me two more** prepares a full fresh eligible pair, avoids the last two pairs' sources and the currently offered photos, and preserves started Adventures in Explore’s Continue. If two fresh options aren't available, the current offer stays. When imported photos match no themed Home plan, Home offers a truthful selection from the available library. In the browser, it offers two different choices from selected files even when there are fewer than ten or those photos were explored before; queued and deleted photos stay out. Small selections may appear in both choices. Equal browser file modification timestamps never collapse separate files into one burst. Smaller native libraries can still choose an Adventure in Explore. Entrance animations respect reduced motion.

**Pit Stops**, discoverable in Explore, use real accessible library photos and illustrated menu cards with optimized square WebP art. Guess the Year and Which Came First use native photo creation dates; browser file-modified dates never power these quizzes. Memory Match works on native and web with four pairs by default or six by choice, remembered locally. Its minimal two/three-column board fits the available screen, with 300 ms card flips, automatic mismatch flip-backs, subtle match feedback and a quiet completion state. Reduced motion, screen readers and the haptics preference are respected. Where Was This is visibly unavailable until reliable location metadata is supported. Games do not write cleanup decisions; **Review these photos** creates a short chosen Adventure of the played photos, with the usual reversible set-aside flow. Native game play and photo loading still require device verification.

Adventure review keeps one navigation model — scroll to browse, swipe to discard — with centered Favorite and Share, a separate trashcan, an original-order moving progress rail, and progressive instructions on your actual photographs. Steps persist, can be skipped, and can be replayed from Help. The original total stays fixed while marked photos leave the active sequence. The discard flight shrinks evenly along its curved path and respects reduced motion; review interactions have no haptics. Discarding the final forward photo completes after its flight, even when earlier photos remain. The pager only loads nearby review photos. Adventures remain still-photo snapshots (Live Photos show stills); existing video collections/playback are unchanged. See [review verification and platform limits](docs/PHOTO_REVIEW_QA.md).

Photo adventures and Pit Stops share screenshot exclusion before sampling, including database limits and capacity counts. Screenshot Sweep remains available. Previously generated mixed adventures are retired without changing their saved review history. Screenshot detection uses iOS PhotoKit subtypes and Android MediaStore directory/album metadata, not image dimensions or filenames; exported/moved images without identifying metadata cannot always be recognized.

Which Came First uses two vertically stacked, uncropped, tappable photographs within the safe area. One answer locks the round, highlights the earlier photo, reveals both dates, and advances after 1.8 seconds. Leaving/backgrounding cancels or pauses the timer; screen-reader users advance manually. 

In the browser, choose **Choose photos** on Home to select files. Browser files are kept in IndexedDB locally and their dates are file-modified dates; photos picked together may share a date, but remain separate photos in Adventures.

Classic timed sessions (Swipe), Smart Clean and the deletion queue remain available from Settings.

## Smart Sessions

Classic timed sessions (from Settings) offer Quick Wins (screenshots and easy candidates), Memory Lane (older unreviewed memories), and Best of the Bunch (validated Pro scan groups with a suggested keeper). Choose 2, 5, or 10 minutes for a bounded batch of up to 12, 30, or 60 items. Quick Wins and Memory Lane remain free; Best never substitutes unrelated photos for missing scan results.

Unfinished sessions resume before a new collection starts. Active review time is saved locally and pauses on other screens, in the background, and while media is unavailable. The timer is a gentle target: expiration never decides or deletes anything. Finish early for a summary and a path to the safe deletion queue. Remaining photos are still eligible for a future session; undo reopens the saved session. Two-step onboarding carries your selected session and duration directly into review.

## Vertical Explore prototype

The original prototype route (`/vertical-explore` without an Adventure) is a free, isolated experiment with up to 200 accessible still photos in a fixed newest-first snapshot. Swipe up to see the next photo without a keep decision; swipe left or right for a provisional experimental discard, which removes the photo from the feed (also when scrolling back) while the rest keep their original order. Tap the photo or Undo to bring back the latest discard, as often as you like: it returns to its original place, from the side it left by, and is shown. Previous revisits. Progress, marks and up to 200 undoable discards persist locally. Restoring an individual photo in Review Discards puts it back in the feed and removes its undo entry; Restore All clears undo history while preserving progress. Restart clears experiment state and replays the same IDs, including unavailable ones. Videos are excluded; Live Photos display stills. Photos already in the standard deletion queue or deletion journals are excluded at selection. The experiment never writes production decisions, copies native originals, uploads photos, or invokes device deletion. Its own collection and full-screen viewer expose only restoration.

A fixed-height, vertically paged FlatList places photos in adjacent clipped page slots and keeps a small neighboring render window. Native scrolling provides upward/downward dragging, interactive peeking and release snapping. Horizontal pans block the native scroll recognizer until they fail on vertical intent; after activation their axis stays horizontal. While you hold a photo sideways it follows your thumb, tilts toward the side you drag it, and shows a Delete label on the uncovered side; either direction discards. Vertical paging behaves like a social feed: the page you land on is shown immediately and saved in the background in order, so rapid flicks and peeks are never blocked or pulled back. Only a sideways discard briefly holds input while the photo flies out; the discarded card is then removed in place and the next photo slides up into its slot (the previous one slides down when none follows), so the feed never shifts under your finger. Identity is the stable photo ID, never a list position. Canceled gestures create no decisions. Next works during image loading; photos advanced before loading or unavailable at the time are separately counted rather than described as explored. Reduced-motion settings shorten motion through Reanimated’s system setting. Browser testing uses user-selected local files. Its pager supports mouse/touch vertical dragging and held peeks on the same scroll surface, with distance/velocity release snapping; wheel/trackpad scrolling remains available. Pointer intent locks to one axis; only vertical drags hold the feed, through their release snap. Bounded DOM polling releases the drag lock if the browser omits its final scroll event or interrupts smooth scrolling. Native gesture feel, iCloud handling, and lifecycle behavior require physical-device verification through Draftbit Publishing; see [the iPhone test checklist](docs/VERTICAL_EXPLORE_QA.md).

## Appearance

Choose System, Light, or Dark in Settings. System follows the current device/browser appearance, including live changes. The preference survives launches. Theme colors cover all screens, tabs, media-review controls, messages, loading states, dialogs and legal pages; native status/navigation controls follow the same appearance.

The browser mirrors only the appearance override in localStorage for the initial page paint; IndexedDB remains authoritative. Theme variables are also applied to the HTML root so body-mounted dialogs inherit their colors. Native root backgrounds use Expo SystemUI; Android navigation styles use Expo NavigationBar. Native appearance and navigation-bar behavior still need device verification through Publishing.

## Native application

Device deletion is verified item by item and journaled so an interrupted deletion is re-checked on the next launch; with limited photo access, items Pictopia can no longer see are never reported as deleted without proof. Use Draftbit **Publishing** for native builds. Expo Go does not contain the custom `PictopiaAnalysis` module or RevenueCat's native purchase implementation. The configured minimums are iOS 15.1 and Android 7.0 / API 24, subject to final build verification and maintained Expo support.

- `app/(tabs)/`: Home, Swipe, Smart Clean, Settings.
- Other routes: onboarding, preview, deletion queue, comparison, paywall, statistics, help, legal disclosures.
- `services/`: platform media, storage, analysis, and purchase adapters; serialized repository mutations.
- `lib/pictopia/`: typed models and deterministic review/analysis logic.
- `modules/pictopia-analysis/`: autolinked Swift/Kotlin local module for streaming original-file SHA-256, tiny-image difference hashes, Laplacian variance, metadata, cancellation, and battery/thermal/memory scan checks.
- Native SQLite schema version 1; browser IndexedDB schema version 1. Legacy database names and hash boundary markers stay stable to preserve existing data after the Pictopia rename. No cleanup backend or user account is needed.
- Theme colors and typography live in `theming/themes/`; UI uses NativeWind and the existing Lucide wrapper.

Photos are referenced, not copied, on native devices. Browser selections are stored locally to support resuming. Adventure hearts update native Photos favorites on iOS and device MediaStore favorites on Android 11+, with system authorization and verification. Android MediaStore favorites do not guarantee Google Photos cloud synchronization. Browser/older Android native favorites are unsupported; classic review’s app-local favorites remain separate. Native sharing uses original-quality temporary iOS exports or authorized Android content URIs; Live Photos share their still resource. Temporary iOS exports are removed after the share sheet dismisses, with crash remnants removed on the next share. Native favorites/sharing need a new Publishing build and real-device validation. Live Photos display their still image; iOS original-content hashes include the paired video. iOS Live Photo motion playback uses a native PhotosUI view and still needs device verification.

## Automated verification

Developer/CI entry points (the Draftbit user does not need a terminal):

- `yarn typecheck`
- `yarn lint`
- `node --test tests/*.test.cjs`
- `node tests/appearance-web.cjs` — appearance switching, persistence, dialogs, dark review and secondary screens; default preview `localhost:61772` or `PREVIEW_URL`.
- `node tests/web.cjs` — set `PREVIEW_URL` to the running preview host, Chromium at `CHROMIUM_PATH` or `/usr/bin/chromium`.
- `node tests/sessions-web.cjs` — onboarding, duration choices, paused timers, exact resumption, early completion, and honest scan empty states.
- `node tests/video.cjs` — real browser-generated video import and playback.

Repository tests use fake IndexedDB; native adapter tests exercise actual host SQLite and explicit SDK test doubles. These are not substitutes for native-device or store sandbox testing. Metadata benchmarks cover 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 50,000 rows on the host SQLite engine, not physical-device performance.
